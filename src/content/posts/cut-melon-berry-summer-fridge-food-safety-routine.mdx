Cut melon and berries are ready-to-eat foods after prep, so summer heat, sink splash, dirty boards, and slow refrigerator return can turn a simple fruit bowl into a food-safety problem. This guide was checked on 2026-06-09 against CDC, FDA, USDA FSIS, and FoodSafety.gov resources. It is not restaurant HACCP guidance; it is a household routine for keeping fruit clean, cold, covered, and separated.

## Quick decision table

| Situation | Safer choice | Mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Whole melon and berries arrive home | Keep them away from raw meat and dirty sink zones | Setting produce beside leaking poultry packages |
| Fruit is washed or cut | Use clean hands, board, knife, and container | Reusing a board or towel from raw-food prep |
| Fruit is served outdoors | Keep portions small and refill from cold storage | Leaving the full bowl warm for the whole meal |
| Power outage or unknown time occurs | Follow official discard guidance | Saving fruit because it still looks fine |

## Topic-specific safety notes

Cut melon and berries become ready-to-eat foods after prep. Keep them away from raw animal foods, dirty sink splash, used towels, phones, and leaking packages. Use clean hands, board, knife, and containers. Put out small serving portions and refill from cold storage. If a power outage, warm picnic delay, off odor, slimy texture, visible mold, or unknown time history appears, use official discard guidance rather than appearance alone.
